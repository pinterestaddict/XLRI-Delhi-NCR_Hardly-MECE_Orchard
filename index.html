Executive summary · Recommendation to Elena Marsh and the Alderbrook board

We recommend a staged route to owning Brightwater

Our advice is to start with a 12-month distribution pilot with Brightwater and secure the right to buy the company at a price agreed today. Alderbrook would commit to the full purchase only once the pilot shows the brand can sell in mainstream grocery. Alongside this, we would put Alderbrook's outlet data to work and give Vital one last, closely managed chance. We would not pursue Marrow Lane at $170M.

What could put the functional target at risk

If Brightwater only reaches the low end of its forecast ($260M) and Vital is discontinued, functional drinks make up 11.8% of revenue, just short of the board's 12%.

Where leverage gets tight

The 2.34x figure assumes about $75M of debt is repaid before closing. Without that, leverage reaches 2.61x, and we would need to defer or pay in shares a further $30-61M of the price.

Most of Alderbrook's revenue sits in categories that have stopped growing

Vital hasn't found its footing, new products take over a year to reach shelves, and the weekly outlet data is only used to plan delivery routes. Question 2 · Deep dive: Vital

Vital deserves one more, carefully staged attempt

In shortVital matters more than its $22M suggests, because in a weak year for Brightwater it is what keeps the functional share above 12%. We would release the $50M budget in three stages and only move to the next once the previous one shows results. If it doesn't improve, its best recipes could move under the Brightwater brand.

Question 3 · Deep dive: synergies

Where the savings would come from, and what they are worth

In shortWe estimate around $18M a year in cost savings by FY29, mostly from bringing can production in-house, using our own delivery network and buying materials together. That is worth roughly $130M today. Because this value depends on Alderbrook's plants and trucks, we should keep it out of the price we offer.

Value today is after 25% tax, phased in over FY27-29, less $35M of one-time costs, discounted at 10%. Co-packers charge 18% more per case, which means moving a case in-house saves about 15% of its cost. Even if every case moved, the saving would top out near $24M a year.

How quickly savings build

Beyond cost savings

  • Revenue: we assume none in the base case; a possible $25M lift from better store targeting is treated as upside
  • Speed: Brightwater launches in 90 days; we aim to bring Alderbrook down from 14 months to about 6
  • Insight: Brightwater's trend team can inform sparkling water and Vital

What could reduce them

  • $30M integration, $12M fees, up to $5M co-packer exit
  • Switching distribution isn't cheap: Keurig Dr Pepper set aside up to $250M to move Ghost
  • Some natural-store loyalists may drift away, and some sales may come at Vital's expense
Question 4 · What would it take to execute?

Keeping what makes Brightwater work after the deal

In shortBrightwater's appeal comes from a small, fast-moving team and a brand its customers trust. We would leave the brand, recipes and product team with the founder, and integrate only the areas where our scale helps: manufacturing, delivery, purchasing and finance. Both of Alderbrook's earlier acquisitions lost sales after integration, so we want to understand why before closing.

Stays with Brightwater

  • Brand, recipes, claims
  • Founder and 5-person trend team
  • Natural stores (65% of sales) and the 90-day process

Moves to Alderbrook

  • Mainstream delivery, in waves
  • Cans for high-volume SKUs
  • Buying, quality, finance, analytics

Question 5 · What role should AI and data play?

Putting Alderbrook's outlet data to work

In shortAlderbrook already collects weekly sales from 110,000 outlets but only uses them to plan routes. With a small analytics team and Brightwater's trend specialists, the same data can tell us where to pilot Brightwater, where shelves run empty, which discounts actually pay off and what to launch next.
